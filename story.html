Our library closed at 9pm.
So we opened a café.

est. 2023

Ember & Oak started with two students, one shared thesis deadline and nowhere to work after the library locked its doors.

How it started

In 2023, Maya and Daniel were finishing their final year. Every night at 9pm the library closed and they were sent out with half-written chapters. The cafés nearby closed even earlier, had two plugs between them and gave you a look if you stayed longer than one coffee.

So they made a list in the back of a notebook: what would the perfect place to study look like? Big tables. A plug at every seat. Coffee that is actually good. Prices a student can pay every day. Open late. That list became Ember & Oak.

“Stay as long as your reading list needs.” That rule is still on the wall by the door.

Why “Ember & Oak”

The tables are made from old oak floorboards saved from the university’s first lecture hall, which was knocked down the year we opened. “Ember” is for the roaster: our beans are roasted every week in small batches by a local roaster ten minutes away, so the coffee you drink was roasted days ago, not months.

The people behind the counter

Most of our baristas are students too. They know what it is like to have three essays due on the same day, and they will happily make your fourth flat white a little stronger. We train every new barista for two weeks before they pour their first drink alone, and we pay above the minimum wage, because good coffee needs people who want to stay.

The croissants come out of the oven at 6am. The filter coffee is refilled for free all day. And yes, the quiet room upstairs really is quiet after 6pm.
